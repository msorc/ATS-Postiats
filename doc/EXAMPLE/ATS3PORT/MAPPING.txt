#
# The mapping from ATS3 (ATS-Xanadu) to ATS2 (ATS/Postiats).
#
# This file records every name that had to be mapped when the
# ATS3 test corpus was ported to ATS2.  It is meant to be
# reused by later ports.
#

(*
** 1. The types
**
** ATS3                     ATS2
** -----------------------  -----------------------------------
** sint                     int
** nint                     int (an [int] that is not negative)
** strn                     string
** bool                     bool
** list(a)                  list_vt(a, n)   (linear, static length)
** optn(a)                  option_vt(a, b)
** a0rf(a)                  arrayptr(a, n)  (an array by reference)
** a1sz(a)                  arrayptr(a, n)  (an array by size)
** a1rf(a)/a1sz(a) as a     list_vt(a, n)  (when a static length
**                           is wanted)
** GSEQ(xs, a)              no counterpart: a sequence is a
**                           list_vt, an arrayptr or a range
** GASQ(xs, a)              no counterpart: see [gasq_bin$search]
**                           in the shim
** lstrm(a)                 stream_vt(a)
** strm(a)                  stream_vt(a)
** cgtz                     charNZ
** dflt                     double
** exn                      exn (the same)
** type(a)                  a viewtype (vt0p) or a type (t0p)
** a:type (a static type)   a:vt0p (a viewtype)
** tuple (a, b)             @(a, b)        (a flat tuple)
** vwtpdef                  vtypedef / a view of a flat tuple
** datatype tree(a)         datavtype tree(a)   (see the note
**                           about datavtypes below)
*)

(*
** 2. The directives
**
** ATS3                     ATS2
** -----------------------  -----------------------------------
** #staload UN = "..."       staload UN = "..."
** #staload _ = "..."        staload "..."
**                          (note: the [staload _ = "..."] form
**                           of ATS3 hides the symbols of the
**                           file in ATS2; the plain [staload
**                           "..."] form is the one to use)
** #include "..."           #include "..."  (the path is
**                           relative to the current directory)
** #symload f with g        overload f with g
** #extern fun              extern fun
** #implfun                 implement
** #impltmp                 implement
** #typedef                 typedef
** #vwtpdef                 vtypedef
** #define                  #define
** #include (C code)        %{ ... %}
*)

(*
** 3. The expressions
**
** ATS3                          ATS2
** ---------------------------   ---------------------------------
** prints (a, b, "\n")           println! (a, b)
** printsln (a, b)              println! (a, b)
** print0s/print1s              println!
** g_print (x)                  print (x)
** g_void (x)                   val () = x
** type (x)                     no counterpart (there is no
**                               run-time type reflection); the
**                               static type is given in a comment
** lam (x) => e                 lam (x) => e
** fix f (x) => e               fix f (x) => e
** let val x = e in b end       let val x = e in b end
** case+ x of | C (y) => e     case+ x of | C (y) => e
** list@(1, 2, 3)               $list_vt{int}(1, 2, 3)
** @ (1, 2)                     @(1, 2)
** x[0] (of a tuple)            x.0
** [x] (of a list)              list_vt_get_at / the [overload []]
** $raise E (x)                 $raise E (x)
** try e with | C (y) => e      try e with ~C (y) => e
*)

(*
** 4. The library
**
** ATS3                     ATS2
** -----------------------  -----------------------------------
** list_ncpy (n, x)         list_make_elt (n, x)
** list (n, f)              list_tabulate (n) (with
**                           [list_tabulate$fopr])
** list_make_fwork (f)      a fold that builds a list_vt
** map (xs, f)              list_vt_map (xs) (with
**                           [list_vt_map$fopr])
** maprev (xs, f)           list_vt_reverse (xs) and then
**                           list_vt_map
** appends (xs, ys)         list_vt_append (xs, ys)
** length (xs)              list_vt_length (xs)
** list_vt_nfun (n, f)      list_tabulate (n)
** list_vt (a)              list_vt (a, n)
** optn_cons (x)            option_vt_some (x)
** optn_vt_cons (x)         option_vt_some (x)
** head (xs)                option_vt_unsome (xs)
** strn_make_ncpy (n, c)    a string literal
** strn (n, f)              a string literal
** strn_reverse (s)         string_reverse (s)   (the shim)
** strn_append (s1, s2)     string_append2 (s1, s2) (the shim)
** prefix (s, n)            string_prefix (s, n) (the shim)
** suffix (s, i)            string_suffix (s, i) (the shim)
** slice (s, i, j)          string_slice (s, i, j) (the shim)
** strn_word$strmize (s)    no counterpart
** strn_line$strmize (s)    no counterpart
** nint_dgt$listize (n)     nint_dgt_str (n)   (the shim is not
**                           needed for this one)
** intrange (l, u)          list_make_intrange (l, u)
** (l, u).foritm (f)        intrange_foreach_env (l, u, env)
** GSEQ (xs).prod ()         gseq_prod (xs)     (the shim)
** GSEQ (xs).sum ()         gseq_sum (xs)      (the shim)
** GSEQ (xs).listize ()     the list_vt itself
** GSEQ (xs).rlistize ()    list_vt_reverse (xs)
** GSEQ (xs).foritm (f)     list_vt_foreach_env (xs, env)
** GSEQ (xs).iforitm (f)   list_vt_iforeach_env (xs, env)
** GSEQ (xs).exists (p)     a fold with a predicate
** GASQ (xs).bin$search (x) gasq_bin$search (A, len, cmp) (the shim)
** gdbg's trace (f, "f")    gdbg_trace (f, x, "f") (the shim)
** range_ibtw (l, u)        list_make_intrange (l, u)
** q2lst_snoc (q, x)        the cons of a list_vt
*)

(*
** 5. What has no counterpart in ATS2
**
** - The [GSEQ] and [GASQ] layers.  In ATS3 a [GSEQ] abstracts
**   over the container, so that the same code works over a
**   list, an array or a string.  In ATS2 the container is not
**   abstracted away: a sequence is a [list_vt], an [arrayptr]
**   or an [intrange].  The shim provides the folds that ATS2
**   does not have ([gseq_prod], [gseq_sum]) and the binary
**   search ([gasq_bin$search]).
**
** - The [gdbg] layer.  The shim provides [gdbg_trace] and
**   [gdbg_trace_str], which print the argument and the result
**   of a call.
**
** - Run-time type reflection ([type (x)]).  The type of a value
**   is given in the comments of the ported programs instead.
**
** - Lists of a length that is only known at run-time.  A
**   [list_vt] has a static length, so a list that is built at
**   run-time has to be a [stream_vt] (which is lazy) or an
**   [arrayptr] (which has a run-time length).  This is why the
**   programs that enumerate all the pairs or all the triples
**   of a list (in the ATS3 test [test06_list000]) are not
**   ported.
**
** - The [datatype] with a non-nullary constructor: it is
**   rejected in ATS2, which has no garbage collector.  A
**   [datavtype] is the ATS2 counterpart, but it is a linear
**   heap-allocated structure whose content can only be read
**   through views, and it cannot be freed (there is no way of
**   doing that outside the prelude).  The programs that build
**   trees (the ATS3 tests [test04_xats2cc] and
**   [test10_xcptn00]) represent the trees by the heights of
**   the subtrees of their nodes instead.
**
** - The CSV parser, the file reader and the object (hash map)
**   library of the ATS3 [xatslib/githwxi]: ATS2 has nothing
**   that corresponds to them in its prelude.  The tests of the
**   ATS3 [xatslib] that need them are not ported.
**
** - The splitting of a string into words and into lines (the
**   ATS3 [strn_word$strmize] and [strn_line$strmize]): the
**   environment of an iteration in ATS2 cannot hold a string
**   (a string is a pointer, and the environment of an
**   iteration has to be a flat viewtype), so the words cannot
**   be collected while the string is traversed.
*)

(*
** 6. The gotchas that cost the most time
**
** - [staload _ = "..."] hides the symbols of the file in ATS2.
**   The form to use is [staload "..."].
**
** - [( ... )] is a single expression and [{ ... }] is a block
**   of [val] declarations; a function body with more than one
**   statement is written [let ... in ... end].
**
** - A [list_vt] is linear: it has to be freed (or consumed by
**   another function).  The operations of the shim take a
**   read-only view of a sequence, so that the sequence can be
**   used more than once.
**
** - The element type of a [list_vt] has to be known statically
**   for the list to be printable; [list_vt_cast{int}] (note the
**   braces) is the way to make it so.
**
** - A static argument is introduced with [{ }] and a template
**   argument with [< >]; the two are not interchangeable.
**
** - A [datavtype] cannot be consumed, and a [datatype] with a
**   non-nullary constructor is rejected.
**
** - A closure that captures variables cannot be returned by a
**   function without giving it a closure type; a closure that
**   captures flat variables is made with [lam@] and is held in
**   variable.
**
** - The [try ... with ...] expression is written
**   [try let ... in ... end with ~C (x) => ...], and the
**   payload type of an exception is written in parentheses:
**   [exception E of (string)].
**
** - An integer literal has a flexible type; when the type
**   cannot be inferred, it has to be given, as in
**   [val n: int = ...].
*)
